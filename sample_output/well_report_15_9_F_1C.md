# Pre-Drill Intelligence Report — 15_9-F-1C
## Volve Field, Block 15/9, Norwegian Continental Shelf

**Report Date:** Current Operations
**Well Role:** Current (Active) Well
**Data Sources:** Log QC Analysis, Formation Top Correlation, Reservoir Drillability Assessment
**Contributing Offset Wells:** 15_9-F-11A, 15_9-F-11B, 15_9-F-1A, 15_9-F-1B

---

### 1. Executive Summary

Well 15_9-F-1C has penetrated a carbonate-dominated Hugin reservoir sequence that is structurally shallower at the Draupne cap rock level but extends to a significantly greater depth (base Hugin at 3,950 m vs. offset average of 3,683 m), confirming an extended HC column with no direct analog in the offset well database. Multiple high-confidence HC-bearing intervals have been identified between 3,350 m and 3,950 m, with the best confirmed reservoir quality at 3,800–3,900 m (RHOB 2.31–2.32 g/cc; RT 911–4,665 ohm·m), representing the primary completion target. A critical structural anomaly at 4,050 m — where offsets show prime HC sandstone but F-1C encounters shale — indicates a possible fault offset or major facies change that must be resolved before any perforation programme is finalised. Log data quality is generally good, though the complete absence of sonic data across the well introduces material uncertainty in mechanical property estimation and pore pressure prediction for the deeper carbonate section.

### 2. Data Quality Assessment

#### 2.1 Overall Assessment
Log quality for 15_9-F-1C is **good across the majority of the logged section**, with stable caliper readings and consistent petrophysical tool responses. The primary systemic limitation is the **complete absence of sonic log data across the entire well**, which constrains mechanical earth modelling, pore pressure estimation, and acoustic impedance calculations. All flagged density anomalies in the 3,450–3,600 m range have been confirmed as **real geological heterogeneity** rather than tool artefacts.

#### 2.2 Interval-Specific Data Reliability

| Depth Interval (m) | Reliability | Key Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| 3,100–3,450 | **Good** | No sonic; density reliable |
| 3,450–3,500 | **Good** | Minor density spike — geological, not instrumental |
| 3,550–3,600 | **Good** | Minor density spike — facies change confirmed |
| 3,750–3,800 | **Degraded** | Gas effect on density tool; use neutron porosity + gas correction |
| 4,050–4,100 | **Poor/Incomplete** | Logging terminated; insufficient data points for statistical analysis |

#### 2.3 Critical Data Gaps
- **Sonic data (full well):** No sonic log run or recorded. Empirical correlations (neutron-density) must substitute for all mechanical property and velocity work. This is the single largest data quality limitation for this well.
- **3,750–3,800 m — Gas Effect (MODERATE):** Bulk density readings are suppressed by gas influx. All density-derived porosity values in this interval should be treated as minimum estimates. Neutron porosity crossplots should be used as the primary porosity input; gas corrections are mandatory before volumetric calculations.
- **4,050–4,100 m — Incomplete Data (CRITICAL):** Log run terminated prematurely. This depth coincides with the most significant structural anomaly in the well (shale where offsets show HC sand). The absence of log data at this exact critical depth is operationally significant.

#### 2.4 Offset Well Data Quality Context
- **15_9-F-11B:** Also missing sonic data across the full logged section (3,200–4,700 m); this appears to be a field-wide data gap for this vintage of wells. A critical gas effect flag exists at 4,700–4,720 m and requires cased-hole log confirmation before use in petrophysical models.
- **15_9-F-11A:** Moderate density tool issues at 3,475–3,525 m due to poor pad contact in unstable shale. Density-derived porosity from this interval in the offset dataset is unreliable; resistivity and gamma ray should be used for lithology correlation only.
- **15_9-F-1A and 15_9-F-1B:** Excellent log quality overall. F-1A shows a gas effect at 3,375–3,425 m that is interpretively meaningful (HC-bearing) rather than a quality concern.

### 3. Formation Top Correlation

#### 3.1 Draupne Formation (Cap Rock)

| Parameter | 15_9-F-1C | Offset Average | Depth Shift |
|---|---|---|---|
| Draupne Top | 3,200 m | 3,333 m | **−133 m (shallower)** |

The Draupne cap rock is encountered 133 m shallower than the offset field average. This is interpreted as **consistent with the structural position of F-1C** relative to the field crest, placing it up-dip of most offset wells. The nearest structural analog is F-1B, which shows a Draupne top approximately 50 m deeper than F-1C. The elevated RT reading within the Draupne at 3,200 m (66 ohm·m) is attributable to a **field-wide pyrite enrichment pattern** and carries no reservoir risk implication.

An anomalous lower Draupne interval at **4,050 m** (CRITICAL deviation) has no offset analog and requires urgent structural interpretation — see Section 4 and Section 6.

#### 3.2 Hugin Formation (Reservoir)

| Parameter | 15_9-F-1C | Offset Average | Depth Shift |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hugin Top | 3,350 m | 3,217 m | **+133 m (deeper)** |
| Hugin Base | 3,950 m | 3,683 m | **+267 m (deeper)** |
| Net Hugin Column | ~600 m | ~466 m | **+134 m extension** |

The Hugin reservoir top in F-1C is encountered 133 m deeper than the offset average, consistent with its structural position below F-1B. More significantly, the **Hugin base is 267 m deeper than the offset average** at 3,950 m, indicating a substantially extended reservoir column with the closest analog being the deep F-11B well. This extended column is classified as MODERATE severity in the deviation analysis but is geologically significant — it implies either a deeper hydrocarbon-water contact than mapped or a fault-extended reservoir segment.

#### 3.3 Transition Zone Anomalies
- **3,250–3,350 m (below Draupne, above confirmed Hugin):** Low-RT interval immediately below the cap suggests a **water-wet or tight sand** in the transition zone. Seal integrity above 3,350 m should be verified.
- **3,300 m:** Anomalously high RT (316 ohm·m) within transitional facies — 67× higher than offset average — suggests a **thin HC-bearing sand within a shaly interval**. This should be evaluated as a secondary target.
- **3,400 m:** RT collapse to 1.32 ohm·m between two confirmed HC intervals, interpreted as a **water-bearing or tight cemented zone** acting as an intra-reservoir barrier.

#### 3.4 Structural Interpretation Note
The depth pattern (shallower Draupne, deeper Hugin base) is internally consistent with F-1C occupying a **structurally elevated but down-dip extended position** on the reservoir flank, potentially intersecting a longer reservoir section along a dipping structure. The extended column from 3,350–3,950 m (600 m gross) is the primary exploration result of this well.

### 4. Reservoir Quality & HC Potential

#### 4.1 Reservoir Character Overview
The Hugin reservoir in F-1C is **carbonate-dominated** with a pervasive limestone PEF signature, in contrast to the sandstone-dominated Hugin encountered in most offset wells. This distinction is critical: while HC potential is confirmed at multiple intervals, the carbonate matrix introduces different flow behaviour, cementing patterns, and completion considerations relative to offset analogs. Reservoir quality is **heterogeneous and generally tighter than offsets** in the shallow Hugin section, improving markedly in the 3,700–3,900 m interval.

#### 4.2 Confirmed HC-Bearing Intervals (Ranked by Quality)

**Tier 1 — Primary Completion Targets:**

**3,800–3,900 m | CRITICAL Priority | Best Confirmed Reservoir**
- F-1C character: RHOB 2.31–2.32 g/cc; RT 911–4,665 ohm·m (at 3,800–3,900 m); GR 20–25 API
- Offset comparison: RHOB 0.24–0.32 g/cc lighter than offsets; RT strongly elevated vs. offset average
- Interpretation: Highest porosity, strongest HC saturation, best net reservoir in the well. Lower density than all offset analogs indicates **superior reservoir quality** at this level. This is the highest-priority perforation interval.

**3,700 m | CRITICAL Priority | Exceptional HC Signal**
- F-1C character: RT 3,989 ohm·m; GR 14.3 API (very clean); RHOB 2.225 g/cc
- Offset comparison: Offset RT average 39.2 ohm·m — F-1C is **100× higher**
- Interpretation: Exceptional HC signal in very clean, porous sand. NPHI of 0.15 is low but GR and RT are compelling. Recommend formation test (MDT/DST) to confirm moveable HC and flow capacity.

**3,350 m | CRITICAL Priority | Tight HC-Bearing — Test Required**
- F-1C character: RT 1,809.8 ohm·m; GR 43.7 API
- Offset comparison: Offset RT average 3.2 ohm·m — offsets show shale at this depth; F-1C shows tight HC-bearing rock
- Interpretation: High-priority anomaly with no offset analog at this depth. Tight matrix will limit flow rate but the HC signal is unambiguous. MDT pressure test recommended before perforation decision.

**Tier 2 — Secondary Targets:**

**3,900 m | MODERATE Priority | High-Confidence HC — Best Analog Match**
- F-1C character: RT 4,665 ohm·m; GR 23.3 API; RHOB 2.32 g/cc
- Offset comparison: Offset RT average 3,589 ohm·m — **strongest analog correlation in the well**
- Interpretation: High-confidence HC interval with direct offset calibration. Include in primary completion programme.

**3,600 m | MODERATE Priority | Moderate HC, Possible Transition Zone**
- F-1C character: RT 300.9 ohm·m; RHOB 2.248 g/cc; GR 25.9 API
- Offset comparison: Offset RT average 3,273 ohm·m — F-1C RT significantly lower
- Interpretation: HC presence confirmed but lower RT may indicate **transition zone or partial saturation**. Evaluate in context of fluid contacts before perforating.

**3,750 m | MODERATE Priority | Moderate HC, Consistent with Offset Trend**
- F-1C character: RT 53 ohm·m; GR 25.9 API; RHOB 2.257 g/cc
- Offset comparison: Broadly consistent with offset RT average of 81 ohm·m
- Interpretation: Moderate HC potential, best-constrained interval against offset data. Include in test plan.

**3,300 m | MODERATE Priority | Tight/Shaly HC — Secondary**
- F-1C character: GR 59.9 API (above clean sand cutoff); RT 316 ohm·m
- Interpretation: High RT likely reflecting tight HC or capillary-bound fluid in shaly matrix. Low completion priority but warrants pressure testing.

#### 4.3 Water-Bearing / Non-Reservoir Intervals

- **3,400 m:** Best porosity in the shallow Hugin section (RHOB 2.345, NPHI 0.228) but RT 1.32 ohm·m confirms **brine-saturated**. Good rock quality without HC fill. Marks a likely intra-reservoir water leg or barriers.
- **3,450 m:** RT 4.5 ohm·m, significantly below offset average of 59.5 ohm·m. **Likely HC-water contact depth** in the shallow Hugin. Do not perforate.
- **3,250 m:** Borderline reservoir quality; limestone PEF; RHOB 2.361 slightly better than offsets but **insufficient for standalone target**.
- **3,500–3,550 m:** Good rock quality (GR 32–40 API, RHOB 2.31–2.35) but RT only 8–9 ohm·m — **likely water-bearing**; consider water contact proximity.
- **3,650 m:** RHOB 2.286, NPHI 0.217 — moderate porosity but RT 4.4 ohm·m is **500× lower than offset average** of 2,278 ohm·m. Extreme RT deviation suggests **fault or seal compartmentalisation**; do not perforate without additional investigation.
- **3,100–3,200 m:** Dense, tight cemented zone (RHOB 2.55–2.56 g/cc; near-zero effective porosity). Confirmed **field-wide cemented barrier / regional seal**. Not a drilling or completion target.

#### 4.4 Critical Anomaly — 4,000–4,050 m
Offset wells show **prime HC reservoir** at this equivalent depth (RT 2,508–16,203 ohm·m; GR 27–42 API). F-1C encounters **shale** (GR 74.6–109.9 API; RHOB 2.43–2.51 g/cc; no HC signature). This is the most significant structural/stratigraphic anomaly in the well and indicates either:
1. A **fault displacement** placing F-1C against non-reservoir rock at this level, or
2. A **major lateral facies change** below the main reservoir column.
**Do not perforate this interval.** Structural reinterpretation incorporating this data point is mandatory.

#### 4.5 HC-Water Contact Summary
Based on resistivity trends, the most likely HC-water contact in the shallow Hugin (3,350–3,500 m section) is approximately **3,450 m**. The deeper Hugin section (3,700–3,950 m) shows a more complex saturation profile with strong HC signals to at least 3,950 m. No field-equivalent water contact has been confirmed for the extended deep column.

### 5. Drillability Forecast

- Basis: RHOB 2.55–2.56 g/cc; dense cemented limestone; near-zero porosity confirmed by NPHI 0.159–0.164

- Basis: GR 107.7 API — shale character; softer than carbonate but plastic
